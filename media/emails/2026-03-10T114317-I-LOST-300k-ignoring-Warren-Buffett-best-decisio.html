Let me show you the math that changed my life.

Let's say right now you make $8,000 per month.

You've got $6,000 in monthly expenses, so you can save $2,000 to invest.

The traditional advice says put that $2,000 into index funds every month.

After 10 years of investing $2,000 per month assuming 10% interest, you've got $415,000.


That might sound like a lot of money.

It's not.

Thats hardly enough to even retire.

Now let's say you aggressively invest that $2,000 into mentors, coaches, courses, and your skills for the next 2 years instead and spend $50,000. 

You increase your earning potential to $100,000 per month (totally realistic with this much investment). 

Of course, you can probably enjoy a much better lifestyle making $100K a month than $8K, but for this example let's say you keep your expenses the same at $6,000.

That means you can now invest $94,000 per month for the last 8 years.

Assuming the same 10% return, you've now got just about $14 million dollars.

Or around 40 times more....


That right there is the difference between the rich and the poor.

The rich invest into their skillset to increase their earning potential first.

And after they're making a bunch more money, then they invest.

Early on in your journey, the best and highest return investment is almost always yourself.

Not stocks.

Not crypto.

Not real estate.

YOU!

Because a 10% return on $2,000 is $200.

But a 10x return on your earning ability is life-changing.

Meanwhile....

They could spend that same energy learning a skill that would 5x their income in 18 months.

I invested everything into learning how to make money.

Courses.

Mentors.

Coaches.

Books.

Events.

But by that point, I had so much more capital to deploy that the returns were actually meaningful.

$2,000 a month into stocks when you're broke is a rounding error.

$2,000 a month into your skillset when you're broke is a rocket ship for your life. 

Choose accordingly.
